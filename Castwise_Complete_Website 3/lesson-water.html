Watercraft lesson

Find fish and read the water

Fish move to find food, shelter, oxygen and comfortable conditions. Learning to read the water helps you choose a sensible place to start.

1. Look for features

Reed edges, overhanging branches, weed beds, drop-offs and changes in bottom depth can all be worth investigating.

2. Watch quietly

Look for dimples, swirls, fish movement or small baitfish. Approach quietly and avoid casting shadows across shallow water.

3. Read the conditions

Wind can push food towards a bank; current seams can carry food in rivers. Temperature, light and water clarity also affect where fish may be.

4. Start close before casting far

Fish sometimes feed close to the bank. Try a few accurate casts to likely features before assuming you need maximum distance.

5. Change one thing at a time

If bites do not come, try a different depth, bait, casting angle or location. Changing one thing helps you learn what made a difference.

Remember: These are clues, not guarantees. Fish behaviour changes with season, weather, pressure and venue.
